# Ifrit

An ifrit is a worker that stays lit while you are there, and that Djinn puts out after a while without you. You ask
the Djinn for one when you want to iterate live: changes you judge by eye on a front, or a stack you want to try by
hand. Between two of your requests it waits, taking no slot and spending no token.

## When the Djinn lights one

The Djinn lights an ifrit when you want to work live with it, never for work a task does alone with its checks: a
fix, a feature or an investigation still goes to a task, cheaper and checked before it ends. One ifrit is lit per
wish. It works in a project, with its own worktree and branch like any task, on Claude Code or Antigravity, and starts
at once, pausing no one.

## Talking to it

You talk to the Djinn, which relays each of your requests to the ifrit, short and concrete; you may also write in the
box under its events. After each request the ifrit makes the change, says in three lines at most what changed and how
to see it, then waits for the next one. The Djinn does not repeat its answer, and asks you a question about it only
for a decision: such a question shows in the ifrit's band.

What lasts, a development server or a whole stack, the ifrit runs through Djinn rather than in the background: Djinn
finds the ports it listens on and shows them as links, and stops it when the ifrit goes out or ends. Its command must
be allowed in the project, like a watcher's.

## In the window

An ifrit shows in vermilion, with a flame. Its band comes first in what waits for you: its state, its links, its
questions, and how long before it goes out once Djinn warned you. Its wish counts the ifrits lit, the load bar counts
them apart, and the status bar counts the gates they hold.

It keeps its [gates](#gates) while it is lit, with no hour limit: they go back when it goes out or ends. Who waits for
one is told it is released when you leave, and at what time at the latest; the Djinn offers you to finish the ifrit or
stop it.

## When you leave

Your keys, clicks and scrolls in the window in front, your keys in a terminal of Djinn, your answers and your own
commands keep the ifrit lit; the Djinn, the workers and a window left untouched do not. After half an hour without
you, a notification warns that it goes out in five minutes, with a button to keep it lit. Then, once its turn under
way ends, Djinn puts it out: its servers stop, its gates and slot go back, its worktree, branch and session stay. You
may set another delay, from five minutes to four hours, for the machine or for one ifrit. A restart of Djinn brings a
lit ifrit back out.

A message lights it again, from the Djinn or yours with Relight on its card: it resumes its session in its worktree,
its servers started first. The Djinn never relights one without your word.

## Finishing it

Once its work is good, Finish on its card, or ask the Djinn: its servers stop, its gates go back, and its work is
brought into the wish's branch like any task's, through the project's checks. Stopping it keeps its work without
bringing it in.
